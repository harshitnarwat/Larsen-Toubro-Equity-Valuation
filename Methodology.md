# Methodology

## 1. Financial Analysis

Historical financial statements are analysed to understand revenue growth, profitability, margins, earnings, working capital, balance-sheet structure and cash-flow trends.

## 2. Profitability Analysis

ROE and ROA are evaluated alongside DuPont decomposition to identify the drivers of shareholder returns.

## 3. Beta & Cost of Equity

Beta is analysed using five-year monthly market data. Comparable-company betas are unlevered where appropriate and used to support the cost-of-equity framework.

The CAPM framework is used to estimate:

**Cost of Equity = Risk-Free Rate + Beta × Equity Risk Premium**

## 4. WACC

WACC combines the cost of equity and after-tax cost of debt using the selected capital structure weights.

The model's base-case WACC is **12.20%**.

## 5. DCF Valuation

The DCF uses an FCFF framework:

**FCFF = EBIT × (1 − Tax Rate) − Reinvestment**

Projected FCFF is discounted using WACC.

Terminal value is calculated using the perpetual-growth approach:

**Terminal Value = FCFFₙ₊₁ / (WACC − Terminal Growth)**

The resulting enterprise value is adjusted for cash and debt to derive equity value and implied value per share.

## 6. Sensitivity Analysis

The DCF includes sensitivity analysis across different WACC and terminal-growth assumptions to illustrate how changes in key inputs affect implied value per share.

## 7. Relative Valuation

Comparable-company analysis uses:

- EV/Revenue
- EV/EBITDA
- P/E

The selected peer multiples are applied to L&T's relevant financial metrics to derive implied enterprise/equity values and implied value per share.

## 8. Football Field

Valuation ranges from relative valuation and DCF scenarios are presented together with the 52-week market range.

## 9. Financial Stability

Altman's Z-Score is used as an additional indicator of financial stability and is tracked historically.

## 10. Market Risk

The workbook includes historical VaR and Monte Carlo simulation to supplement the fundamental valuation analysis.

---

## Interpretation

The project intentionally compares multiple valuation approaches rather than relying on a single intrinsic-value estimate. Differences between DCF and relative valuation are interpreted in the context of their respective assumptions, market multiples and sensitivity to inputs.
